# V8 theoretical contributions and evidentiary limits

## What the extension actually identifies

The extension evaluates specific operational admission, review, restriction and correction policies under explicit simulation assumptions. It strengthens the distinction already made in the manuscript between temporal dependence in governance and incremental value from a sequential learner. Thirty independent paired training blocks replace the original three-run sensitivity as the basis for estimating training variability. A prospective seed-audit amendment retains the historical three groups separately because their episode streams overlap across algorithms and training groups; the primary thirty blocks have disjoint episode-seed unions across algorithms. The exact V7 stratum preserves the historical comparison; the corrected stratum separates robustness from two necessary implementation corrections.

In the corrected A stratum, Q-learning had a negative mean reward contrast against the bandit in 12 of twelve cells. There were 12 negative and 0 positive Holm-adjusted primary results. In the crossed-bootstrap sensitivity, 12 intervals were entirely negative and 0 entirely positive. These counts refer to different uncertainty calculations and must not be collapsed into a universal claim of superiority. They concern the tested tabular Q-learning and immediate-reward bandit, with a secondary linear approximation, under finite horizons and fixed budgets.

The feedback intervention produced 0 relative improvements and 0 relative deteriorations after Holm adjustment across six primary contrasts, conditional on the fixed evaluation panel. The observability intervention produced 0 relative improvements and 0 relative deteriorations after Holm adjustment across six primary contrasts, conditional on the fixed evaluation panel. Across the twelve B/C condition–scenario cells, Q-learning had 0 positive absolute mean contrasts against the bandit. Those point estimates are descriptive, and the interaction tests answer a different question from absolute policy superiority. A change in the Q-minus-bandit gap does not, by itself, identify conditions in which Q is better.

## What the mechanism tests did not establish

Neither feedback-report loss nor administrative observability produced a Holm-adjusted interaction in the current data (zero of six in each family). Enrichment reduced the Q-learning deficit descriptively in all three scenarios, but none of those changes was statistically established. The reduced-observation reference contrast had a negative pointwise t interval, yet its Holm-adjusted p-value was 0.1435 and its crossed-bootstrap interval included zero. It must therefore not be elevated to a confirmed mechanism result. The experiments strengthen robustness but do not establish that the tested information limitations explain the Q-learning disadvantage. Absence of a significant interaction is not evidence of equivalence.

## Feedback: a tested teaching-signal mechanism with a narrow scope

The B experiment intervenes on the availability of scalar reward-component reports while leaving the physical transition mechanism, observation channels and common evaluation objective fixed. It therefore tests whether this form of missing teaching information changes relative policy performance. It does not directly manipulate incident detection, the existence of appeals, reporting delay, accountability practices, or organizational ability to connect individual outcomes to originating decisions.

Zero encoding matters theoretically: stochastic omission changes the scale and realized composition of the learning target, not only its noise. Conditional on a transition, the expected bandit teaching reward is (1?p) times current-action reward. The expected Q teaching reward is (1?p) times the non-guard transition reward plus the unchanged guard penalty. With normally zero guard penalties, this is proportional reward scaling in expectation, not a different ideal governance preference ordering. Any finite-budget interaction can therefore reflect learning variance, initialization, TD propagation and (for linear Q) clipping as well as reward scale. State information can also reveal consequences whose reward report is absent. Consequently, even a nonzero interaction supports a claim about report availability under this training rule; it does not establish that all forms of organizational feedback quality produce the same effect. The bandit remains myopic and does not reattribute delayed review rewards, limiting generalization to other contextual-bandit designs.

## Observability: administrative information and representational complexity

The C experiment manipulates queue/capacity channels and administrative timing information for existing jobs. It does not add true harmfulness, future arrivals or future random outcomes. Its primary policy comparison shares the same information and tabular representation in each condition. The enrichment increases the possible tabular space by at most two; reduced observation collapses distinctions. The linear diagnostic uses a richer raw feature vector and cannot be read as an information-matched test of tabular learning.

The intervention identifies the combined consequence of the specified information design and its learning complexity. It does not separately estimate a pure information benefit holding state-space sparsity constant. Visited states, state-actions and unseen evaluation-state rates are therefore part of the evidence, not a post hoc explanation treated as proven. V7's inherited outcome aggregates remain privileged and uncalibrated proxies for what a platform operator could observe.

## Boundary-resource governance: implication rather than direct empirical test

The mechanism-level contribution concerns repeated operational decisions mediated by admission systems, review queues, enforcement and appeals. Such arrangements can connect present scrutiny to later publication, incidents, restrictions and participation. The V8 interventions make two candidate boundary conditions empirically examinable within the simulation: availability of learning reports and representation of administrative state.

This narrows the manuscript's claim. It does not model the evolution of APIs/SDKs, distributed negotiation over boundary resources, or changes in platform strategy. It does not show that adding an appeal channel or a traceability system improves a real commercial ecosystem. Rather, it provides a controlled test of whether particular information channels alter a policy contrast when those operational mechanisms already exist. The proposed organizational implication is to evaluate the informational and operational arrangement jointly with the learner; it remains a theoretical extrapolation requiring platform-level evidence.

## Multidimensional governance and adverse findings

Reward weights encode assumptions, not a universal welfare function. Contributions accepted, harmful releases, incidents, unjustified restrictions, review work and governance expenditure remain distinct. A negative reward contrast cannot establish complete governance dominance if other outcomes trade off. Likewise, zero safety interventions are expected when action masks prevent illegal proposals and do not demonstrate zero security risk. Participation affects submission composition in this model, not submission volume; claims about real developer exit or innovation supply would exceed the implemented mechanism.

The corrected balanced sequential scenario illustrates the trade-offs descriptively: Q-learning accepted 0.71 more legitimate contributions and imposed 1.13 fewer unjustified restrictions per episode, while consuming 5.08 additional human-review actions and 2.49 additional expenditure units. These secondary, pointwise estimates qualify the reward ranking; they do not establish a universally preferable policy.

## Strength of the contribution for a revised submission

The extension improves methodological credibility through independent training replication, implementation diagnosis, prospective local specification, complete reporting, paired mechanism contrasts and reproducible uncertainty estimates. This is an improvement over motivating feedback/observability explanations solely from descriptive convergence diagnostics, but its benefit is primarily methodological and evidentiary: the proposed moderators were tested without being confirmed. They should remain tentative theoretical possibilities, not demonstrated explanations. Its theoretical contribution is strongest when stated as conditional evidence about the specific informational mechanisms tested, with the full results and adverse findings retained.

It does not by itself establish a positive sequential-value boundary, validate the simulator on commercial platforms, or guarantee editorial fit for Electronic Commerce Research. If no reliable absolute Q advantage is present, the manuscript should say that the tested manipulations did not locate such a boundary. No factorial feedback-by-observability interaction was run: the separate interventions already have restricted meanings, and a factorial expansion would not resolve zero-imputation and representation-complexity ambiguities. Additional algorithm tuning or factorial multiplication would require a separate question and protocol amendment.

## Evidence classification

| Statement | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| Thirty runs and ten evaluations per learned cell; exact archived reproduction; source preservation | Numerically verified |
| Primary reward contrasts and condition interactions | Statistical estimates conditional on the fixed evaluation panel; Holm families specified prospectively |
| Crossed-bootstrap intervals | Secondary sensitivity to training and evaluation draws, based on only ten evaluation seeds |
| Operational outcome contrasts and linear-Q rankings | Secondary estimates/descriptive comparisons with pointwise uncertainty |
| Report-availability and administrative-information interventions | Mechanisms directly manipulated, within the stated implementation |
| Feedback traceability, accountability or boundary-resource redesign on real platforms | Theoretical implications, not directly tested |
| Intrinsic inferiority of RL, or a universal platform policy recommendation | Not established |
